# Puzzle Bible

Source of truth for all room, item, clue, decoy, and hint content. Dad's name is **David**. The protagonist's name is left unspoken (he is "you").

## Global notes
- The house is Dad's childhood home, frozen in the 1980s. Dad was born in 1976.
- Plant every hiding place in a room description as a throwaway line. Never hide a key in something the player was never told exists.
- The pencil from the basement is never consumed and returns in the ending.
- Keep each room to about five decoys.
- Product references stay generic where a real person is involved (for example, "an aerobics workout tape").

## Key chain
Basement key (hallway door) -> Hallway key (bedroom door) -> Bedroom key (study door) -> Study key (grandparents' door) -> Grandparents' key (front door).
Note: each puzzle's reward is named for the door it opens: `hallwayKey`, `bedroomKey`, `studyKey`, `grandparentsKey`, `frontDoorKey`.

## Room 1: Basement (tutorial: use item on item)

**Description plants:** a workbench with a coffee can of pencils, a cassette with its tape pulled out, a Walkman, a wood-grain Atari console hooked to a small TV, an Atari cartridge box, a Simon game, a Magic 8-Ball, a shoebox of baseball cards, a View-Master, a jar of mismatched batteries.

**Puzzle flow**
1. Examine cassette: the tape is tangled.
2. `REWIND TAPE WITH PENCIL` (accept `FIX TAPE`, `WIND TAPE`): tape rewound. Narrator gripe about technology at warmth 0.
3. `INSERT TAPE` / `LOAD WALKMAN`, then `PLAY WALKMAN`. Optional gate: batteries (the jar contains exactly one working pair).
4. Dad's recording plays. Dad's recorded message (paraphrase, write final text in his voice): he tells the story of the first video game Easter egg, and says the best secrets are under the things you do not think to look under.
5. `SEARCH CARTRIDGE BOX` then `LIFT FOAM` / `SEARCH UNDER FOAM`: the key is there. Reward: `hallwayKey`.

**Clues:** Dad's recording; the Atari console with wood paneling; a label on the box in kid handwriting ("DO NOT LOSE. MOM WILL KILL ME."); a faded Atari catalog with a folded corner; a joystick with a rubber band.

**Decoys:** Magic 8-Ball ("Ask again later. Thrilling."); Simon ("Four colors and a sense of betrayal. No key."); shoebox of baseball cards ("Mint condition, probably worth less than the box."); View-Master ("Three-dimensional disappointment."); battery jar (decoy or gate).

**Hints**
1. "That tape is not going to fix itself. What would a kid in the 80s use?"
2. "A pencil. There's a coffee can of them. Try rewinding."
3. "After the tape plays, think about where Dad said to look. Under things."

**Warmth:** 0 on start. 1 after the key is found.

## Room 2: Hallway (teaches: interact with a machine, deduce from a message)

**Description plants:** a phone table with a rotary phone, an answering machine with a blinking light, Grandpa's pager, a coat closet slightly ajar with a sleeve showing, a snapshot of young Dad in a jacket with the collar popped, a scrap of paper by the phone ("TELL MOM: NOT MY JACKET."), a bowl of labeled keys, a Yellow Pages, an umbrella stand, a wall calendar stuck on a month in 1987.

**Puzzle flow**
1. `EXAMINE PAGER`: the screen shows a seven-digit number. Use a fictional number: **555-0142**. Narrator: the beeper is for when people needed to find you but not hear your voice. (The pager is Grandpa's work pager, not Dad's.)
2. `DIAL 5550142` (accept `555-0142`, `DIAL NUMBER`): rotary clicks, ring, then the answering machine plays.
3. Wrong numbers: busy signal, stranger's voice, Grandma's old outgoing greeting. Each gets its own quip. Never a dead end.
4. The message: ten-year-old Dad leaves a message for his mom. He is at a friend's, reminds her "the thing" is in his lucky jacket, and refuses to say which pocket because she snoops. **Silence beat after.**
5. `OPEN CLOSET`, `SEARCH JACKET`: outer pocket gives an emergency quarter (decoy, a callback: "Always keep an emergency quarter."). `SEARCH INSIDE POCKET` / `UNZIP POCKET` gives the key. Reward: `bedroomKey`.

**Clues:** the answering machine message; the ajar closet; the snapshot; the scrap of paper; Dad's recalled line ("a good jacket has a secret pocket for a secret plan").

**Decoys:** bowl of keys with seven keys, none fit, each labeled in Grandma's handwriting ("Nobody ever labeled the right one."); Yellow Pages ("Too heavy to be useful and too light to throw."); umbrella stand ("No key. No dignity."); wall calendar ("Time stopped. Very dramatic."); Rolodex ("Six hundred names. None of them answer.").

**Hints**
1. "The beeper wants something. Who calls a beeper?"
2. "There's a phone right there. It has a dial. Use it."
3. "Dad said he put it in his lucky jacket. Where do jackets live?"

**Warmth:** rises to 2 after the message and the silence beat.

## Room 3: Dad's Boyhood Bedroom (teaches: the odd verb is right)

**Description plants:** an NES with one controller, a Tecmo Bowl cartridge on the shelf, a toybox with a hand-lettered lid ("AUTOBOTS ONLY. DECEPTICONS KEEP OUT."), a Polaroid on the wall of young Dad holding an Optimus Prime trailer like a trophy, a comic or sticker sheet on the bed, a few decoy toys.

**Puzzle flow**
1. `INSERT CARTRIDGE`, `PRESS POWER` (accept `TURN ON NES`): the screen flickers and fails. Narrator mocks.
2. `BLOW ON CARTRIDGE` (accept `BLOW INTO CARTRIDGE`), then `INSERT CARTRIDGE` again: the game boots.
3. Title card: an **ASCII art homage** of a robot-leader shape plus the words "THE TOUCH". Do not reproduce any song lyrics. The narrator can say "I'm not going to say it. You know it." Treat the art as a stylized homage, not an official logo; swap to a generic robot face if you are concerned about trademarks.
4. `OPEN TOYBOX` becomes available after the title card. `SEARCH TOYBOX`, `TAKE TOY`, `EXAMINE [toy]` for each decoy.
5. `UNFOLD TRAILER` then `SEARCH COMPARTMENT`: the key is inside. Reward: `studyKey`.

**Clues:** the title card; the lid label; the Polaroid; Dad's recalled line ("The leader never leaves the Matrix unguarded", used as a franchise nod, not a lyric); the comic on the bed.

**Decoys (about five):** Cabbage Patch Kid ("Adopted. No key in the paperwork."); Rubik's Cube ("Unsolved since 1983."); Etch A Sketch ("A drawing of a stick figure. Hang it in the Louvre."); G.I. Joe figure missing an arm ("He's lost more than a key."); a villain robot toy ("Hostile. Probably holding a grudge.").

**Hints**
1. "That cartridge has been through a lot. What did every kid do to them?"
2. "Blow on it. Yes, really."
3. "The title card shows you who to look for. The toybox lid agrees."

**Warmth:** stays 2 here with warm moments. Rises only at the Study.

## Room 4: Study (teaches: do the "wrong" thing on purpose)

**Description plants:** a hi-fi console with a turntable (cord unplugged behind a bookshelf), a record on the platter, a desk with a Commodore 64 and a 5.25" floppy disk box, a sticky note on the monitor in Grandpa's handwriting ("DO NOT TOUCH MY DISKS."), a newspaper clipping about the backmasking scare with a circled paragraph and a margin note ("DAVID. NO."), a typewriter, a stack of vinyl, a desk lamp, a globe, a letter opener.

**Puzzle flow**
1. `FIND CORD` / `LOOK BEHIND BOOKSHELF`, then `PLUG IN TURNTABLE`: the platter spins.
2. `PLAY RECORD`: unintelligible audio. Narrator quip about a cat on a keyboard.
3. The clipping gives the idea. `STOP RECORD`, then `TURN RECORD COUNTERCLOCKWISE` (accept `SPIN BACKWARDS`, `REVERSE RECORD`, `ROTATE RECORD BACKWARD`).
4. The reversed message: young Dad's voice, proud of himself, says he hid the key where Grandpa would never dare to look. The reversed audio is made by reversing the recorded buffer (record Dad's line forward as the "reversed" original, and test both directions). The record is a homemade novelty disc, not a real band's album, to avoid lyrics. **Silence beat after. Warmth jumps to 3.**
5. `OPEN DISK BOX`: the key is taped inside the lid. Reward: `grandparentsKey`.

**Clues:** the reversed message; the clipping; the C64; the sticky note; Dad's recalled line ("The best hiding place is wherever the owner is most afraid you'll look").

**Decoys:** typewriter ("A keyboard that fights back. No key."); vinyl stack ("Every one of them is a snooze."); desk lamp ("Illuminating, but not useful."); globe ("Geopolitics is out of date. So is this."); letter opener ("Opens letters. Sadly not mysteries.").

**Recovery:** over-spinning "scratches" the record, but `FLIP RECORD` always restores it. Never a dead end.

**Hints**
1. "That record sounds like nonsense. What if it's not nonsense?"
2. "The clipping is circled for a reason. Try the record the other way."
3. "After the message, who was the one thing Grandpa told Dad not to touch?"

**Warmth:** 3 after the silence beat.

## Room 5: Grandparents' Bedroom (the emotional finale)

**Description plants:** a VCR under a TV with a blinking 12:00, a clock radio on the nightstand stopped at an odd time, a nightstand drawer with a scribbled TV listing ("SATURDAY" circled), a shelf with an aerobics workout tape case, a Betamax tape, a ballerina jewelry box, leg warmers, a tie rack.

**Puzzle flow**
1. `OPEN TAPE CASE`: a tape with a handwritten label ("DAD'S CHRISTMAS 1986"). The case is the decoy-lookalike, so the label is the reveal.
2. `INSERT TAPE`, `PRESS PLAY`: the picture is fuzzy.
3. `ADJUST TRACKING` (accept `FIX TRACKING`, `TRACKING`): static clears step by step.
4. Optional gag: `SET CLOCK`. Narrator: "A generation of households, united in not knowing how to set the clock." This is the last big laugh and must come before the video. Never required.
5. The video: Christmas morning 1986. Ten-year-old Dad unwraps an NES. Grandpa narrates off-camera (he is the early adopter with the camcorder). Dad sneaks into this room and whispers where he is hiding something: "Saturday morning is the best time to find it." The last seconds glitch to a single sincere sentence from adult Dad. **Final silence beat. Warmth 4.**
6. `OPEN BATTERY COVER` (accept `REMOVE BATTERY COVER`, `SEARCH CLOCK RADIO`) on the clock radio: the key. Reward: `frontDoorKey`.
7. `UNLOCK DOOR`: the alarm blares.

**Clues:** the video; the clock radio; the TV listing; Dad's recalled line ("He set his alarm for 6 a.m. on Saturdays to beat everyone to the cartoons"); the narrator's snooze-button joke.

**Decoys:** aerobics tape case ("Dad's mom exercised. Dad's mom was weirdly fit. No key."); jewelry box ("Ballerina is judging you."); Betamax tape ("Wrong format. Wrong era. Wrong decade, honestly."); leg warmers ("No pockets and no shame."); tie rack ("Eleven ties. None of them taste.").

**Recovery:** ejecting the tape early resets tracking but returns the tape to the player.

**Hints**
1. "The VCR is blinking at you. It wants a tape."
2. "The picture is a mess. VCRs had a control for that."
3. "After the video, think about what Dad did in this room. Saturday, 6 a.m."

---

## Ending
1. `UNLOCK DOOR` (or `OPEN FRONT DOOR` with the key): the alarm sound starts.
2. Black screen, a pause.
3. Wake-up scene: plain text, no jokes. The protagonist wakes in his own bed, thinks fondly of his dad.
4. He finds a pencil in his pocket.
5. End. Nothing after the pencil.

## Narration banks (minimum)
- 8 variants for "unknown verb" at each warmth level.
- 8 variants for "can't do that" at each warmth level.
- 5 variants for "already did that" at each warmth level.
- Each decoy needs one unique line (above), plus a second line for warmth 3 and 4 if the player revisits the room late.
