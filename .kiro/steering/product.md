# Product Overview

## Working title
"Outdated: A Text Adventure"

## One-line pitch
An Infocom-style, text-only adventure about a 16-year-old who mocked his late father's love of 80s technology, and who now has to use that technology to escape a dream version of his father's childhood home.

## Premise
- The protagonist is 16. His father, David (born 1976), died a couple of years ago. They had a good relationship.
- David was a kid in the 1980s (ages 4 to 13). He told his son endless stories about his childhood gadgets and toys. The protagonist teased him that all of it was junk.
- The game is a dream. The protagonist wakes in the basement of his dad's childhood home, preserved in 1980s amber, and must unlock his way out.
- Every puzzle uses an 80s gadget to deliver a clue. Every key is hidden inside a toy or object that was exclusive to the 80s.
- Every clue is something Dad once told him, which the protagonist never fully experienced.

## Structure
Five rooms, five puzzles, five keys, in this order:
1. Basement -> key opens the Hallway
2. Hallway -> key opens Dad's Boyhood Bedroom
3. Dad's Boyhood Bedroom -> key opens the Study
4. Study -> key opens the Grandparents' Bedroom
5. Grandparents' Bedroom -> key opens the Front Door -> ending

## Emotional arc
The narrator starts as a sarcastic voice that mocks the old tech (the protagonist's armor). Each solved puzzle lowers the narrator's guard (see narrator-voice.md, "Warmth Ladder"). By the end the narrator is sincere. The game ends when the protagonist wakes up, thinks fondly of his dad, and finds a pencil from the first puzzle in his pocket. Then it ends. Do not add anything after that.

## Audience and goals
- Judges and players who may never have played a text adventure. Onboarding must be gentle (see HELP and HINT in the spec).
- A complete, finishable game that is better than a larger, half-built one.
- Authentic 1980s details, with emotional payoff over cleverness.

## Hard content rules
- Never reproduce song lyrics, even short ones. Evoke songs by mood or title only.
- No real named people or celebrities. Describe products generically (for example, "an aerobics workout tape").
- The narrator may mock objects and the protagonist's own cynicism. The narrator never mocks Dad.
- Grief is handled sincerely. No jokes at the expense of death.
